Flour types for bread

Strength. The strength of a flour comes from its protein, which forms gluten
when it gets wet. Bread flour has 12 to 14 % protein; plain flour has about
10 %. In Spain the strength is written as W: a W of 300 or more is a strong
flour for long proofs.

Whole wheat flour keeps the bran and the germ. It absorbs more water, so a
dough with half whole wheat needs about 5 % more water.

Rye flour has little gluten. A loaf with a lot of rye is dense and moist, and it
is baked in a tin.

Spelt flour is an old wheat with a weaker gluten. Mix it gently and do not over
knead it.

Store flour in a closed container in a cool, dark place. Whole grain flours go
rancid sooner, in about three months.
