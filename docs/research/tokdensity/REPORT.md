# How many characters a token holds

Research for [#26](https://github.com/javierponferradalopez/local-semantic-search/issues/26). Measured on 2026-09-19.

## Method

- Only tokenizers were loaded, with `AutoTokenizer.from_pretrained(id)`. No weights, no pipeline.
- Library: `@huggingface/transformers` 4.3.0 on Node v24.11.0 (the same library version as the [embedder study](https://github.com/javierponferradalopez/local-semantic-search/blob/research/text-embedder/docs/research/embedlab/REPORT.md)).
- Tokens were counted with `add_special_tokens: false`. Characters are Unicode code points (`Array.from(s).length`). The UTF-16 length is also recorded; it is equal to the code-point count for every sample in this corpus.
- Corpus: 32 samples in `corpus/`, written for this study. Five samples per kind, 1550–2695 characters each. The two pathological samples are generated by `measure.mjs` from a seeded generator, so each run sees the same strings.
- The runner is `measure.mjs`. The raw output is `out.jsonl` (one JSON object per line, `type` = `run`, `sample`, `tokenizer`, `measure`, `kind`, `cap`, `pdf`).
- The 512-token limit is applied to all three tokenizers, as the ticket asks.

Two readings for the decision table:

1. **Ratio**: tokens predicted for a cap of N characters = `ceil(N / min chars-per-token of the kind)`. The "largest character count that never crosses the wall" is `floor(wall × min chars-per-token)`.
2. **Direct**: the first N code points of every sample that is at least N long are tokenized. The worst sample is the maximum. This catches a dense start that the whole-sample ratio hides.

A cell in the decision table says **fits** only when both readings stay at or under the wall. Where no sample is long enough for a direct reading, the cell carries the ratio only and is marked with `*`.

## Tokenizers loaded

All three loaded. No errors.

| Key | Id | Class | `model_max_length` | Special tokens added by default | Prefix | Wall |
|-----|----|-------|--------------------|-------------------------------|--------|------|
| e5 | `Xenova/multilingual-e5-small` | XLMRobertaTokenizer (SentencePiece) | 512 | 2 (`<s>`, `</s>`) | `passage: ` = 2 tokens in front of text (3 alone) | 512 − 2 − 2 = **508** |
| granite | `ibm-granite/granite-embedding-97m-multilingual-r2` | PreTrainedTokenizer (byte-level BPE) | 32768 | 2 (`<|startoftext|>`, `<|return|>`) | none | 512 − 2 = **510** |
| minilm | `Xenova/all-MiniLM-L6-v2` | BertTokenizer (WordPiece) | 512 | 2 (`[CLS]`, `[SEP]`) | none | 512 − 2 = **510** |

The e5 prefix `passage: ` tokenizes alone as `▁passage`, `:`, `▁` (3 tokens). In front of text the trailing space merges into the next word: `passage: La casa` → `▁passage`, `:`, `▁La`, `▁casa`. The delta against the same text without the prefix was 2 tokens for all ten prose samples. The wall uses 2.

Granite's tokenizer reports `model_max_length` 32768. This study still uses 512 for it, as the ticket frames the question. The number is reported as measured; nothing about the Granite model's real context was tested here.

The MiniLM pathological row has no meaningful mean. The 3000-digit string became **one token**, `[UNK]`: BERT WordPiece replaces any whitespace-delimited word longer than 100 characters with `[UNK]`. The string does not cross the wall; it disappears. The base64-like string is split by BERT's basic tokenizer at `+` and `/`, so most runs are shorter than 100 characters and get WordPieced; three runs were longer and became `[UNK]`. The `safe` value 833 comes from the base64 sample only.

Digits: e5 groups digits into pieces of 1–3 (`20`, `260`, `305`); Granite splits into pieces of up to 3 (`202`, `603`, `05`) and puts every `.`, `,` and the space before `€` in its own token; MiniLM splits `20260305` into `202 ##60 ##30 ##5`. This is why the numbers table is the worst kind for Granite (1.61 chars/token on the weather table) while it is a middling kind for e5.

### Ratio and direct readings disagree in two places that matter

The ratio reading is a linear extrapolation from the whole sample. The direct reading tokenizes the first N characters. In two cells the ratio says fits and the direct reading says breaks:

- minilm, PDF-extracted text, cap 1500: ratio 507, direct 513 (wall 510). The dense start of `pdf/05` (a Spanish meeting record with numbers) is over the wall before the whole-sample ratio would predict.
- minilm, Markdown, cap 1000 and 1200: ratio 428 / 513, direct 465 / 544. Direct is worse at both caps; the table already shows 1200 as a break either way.

In the other direction, the direct reading is often lower than the ratio for code and numbers (a file starts with imports or a heading, which are denser or lighter than the body). The table takes the worse one in every cell.

## Newlines and line-end hyphens in PDF text

The five PDF-like samples have 32–37 newlines and 10–16 hyphenated line breaks (`docu-\nmento`) each. Three variants were tokenized: as-is, `\n` → space, hyphen+newline removed (the word is rejoined), and both.

| Tokenizer | Sample | newlines | hyphen breaks | tokens as-is | Δ newline→space | Δ hyphen joined | Δ both |
|-----------|--------|----------|---------------|--------------|-----------------|-----------------|--------|
| e5 | pdf/01 | 32 | 13 | 447 | 0 | −33 | −33 |
| e5 | pdf/02 | 33 | 12 | 517 | 0 | −27 | −27 |
| e5 | pdf/03 | 35 | 16 | 520 | 0 | −38 | −38 |
| e5 | pdf/04 | 32 | 10 | 500 | 0 | −22 | −22 |
| e5 | pdf/05 | 37 | 11 | 596 | 0 | −28 | −28 |
| granite | pdf/01 | 32 | 13 | 450 | −8 | −28 | −41 |
| granite | pdf/02 | 33 | 12 | 474 | −18 | −25 | −45 |
| granite | pdf/03 | 35 | 16 | 533 | −11 | −40 | −52 |
| granite | pdf/04 | 32 | 10 | 469 | −18 | −22 | −41 |
| granite | pdf/05 | 37 | 11 | 635 | −24 | −26 | −52 |
| minilm | pdf/01 | 32 | 13 | 651 | 0 | −23 | −23 |
| minilm | pdf/02 | 33 | 12 | 466 | 0 | −35 | −35 |
| minilm | pdf/03 | 35 | 16 | 706 | 0 | −30 | −30 |
| minilm | pdf/04 | 32 | 10 | 460 | 0 | −27 | −27 |
| minilm | pdf/05 | 37 | 11 | 823 | 0 | −19 | −19 |

Findings for e5:

- **Newlines cost nothing.** Replacing every `\n` with a space changed the count by 0 in all five samples. SentencePiece treats a newline as whitespace.
- **A line-end hyphen costs 2.2–2.5 tokens.** Joining the hyphenated words removed 22–38 tokens per sample, which is 4.4–7.4 % of the sample. The split word `docu-\nmento` tokenizes as `do`, `cu`, `-`, `▁`, `mento` (5 tokens); the whole word `documento` is 1–2 tokens.

For the other two: MiniLM behaves like e5 on newlines (0) and hyphens (−19 to −35). Granite is a byte-level BPE where a newline is part of the token stream: `\n` → space saved 8–24 tokens per sample (0.25–0.65 tokens per newline; some newlines merge with the next word), and hyphen joining saved 22–40 more.

## What the grilling can read from this

Facts only, as measured on this corpus.

- **Walls.** e5: 508 tokens for a passage (512 − 2 special − 2 for `passage: `). Granite: 510. MiniLM: 510.
- **Mean characters per token, prose.** e5: 4.15 Spanish, 4.28 English. Granite: 4.36 Spanish, 4.81 English. MiniLM: 3.05 Spanish, 4.79 English. The English WordPiece is the outlier on Spanish: it pays about 1.4× the tokens of e5 for the same Spanish text.
- **Largest cap in the set that is safe for prose in all three tokenizers: 1500 characters** (Spanish and English prose, both readings). The tightest cell is MiniLM on Spanish prose at 1500: 505 tokens against a wall of 510, a margin of 5 tokens. The ratio-derived ceiling for Spanish prose on MiniLM is 1521 characters. If PDF-extracted prose is counted as prose, the largest safe cap in the set drops to **1200**: MiniLM on `pdf/05` reaches 513 tokens at 1500 characters.
- **For e5 alone**, the ratio-derived ceilings are 1943 (Spanish prose), 2049 (English prose), 2019 (PDF text), 1668 (numbers), 1297 (Markdown), 1275 (code). The largest cap in the set that is safe for every non-pathological kind on e5 is 1200 characters (worst cell: code, 479 of 508). At 1500, Markdown (586) and code (598) break.
- **Kinds that break first.** The no-space string breaks at 800 on all three (and MiniLM silently turns a long digit string into one `[UNK]`). Among real content: numbers tables on Granite at 1000 (622); Markdown on MiniLM at 1200 (544); Markdown and code on e5 at 1500; numbers on e5 at 1800. Prose is the last to break: Spanish on MiniLM at 1800, Spanish on e5 at 2000, everything at 2500.
- **Largest cap safe for every non-pathological kind on every tokenizer: 800 characters.** At 1000, Granite's numbers table is already over.
- **Newlines** are free for e5 and MiniLM and cost 0.25–0.65 tokens each on Granite. **Line-end hyphens** cost 2.2–2.5 tokens each on e5 (4–7 % of a PDF page's tokens).
