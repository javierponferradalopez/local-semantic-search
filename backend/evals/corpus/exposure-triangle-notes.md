# The exposure triangle

Three settings decide how bright a photo is. When I change one, I must change
another to keep the same light.

## Aperture

The f-number. A small number (f/1.8) opens the lens wide: more light, and a
shallow depth of field. For a portrait with a soft, blurred background, I use
f/1.8 or f/2.8 with my 35 mm lens, and I stand close to the person. For a
landscape that is sharp from front to back, f/8 to f/11.

## Shutter speed

How long the sensor sees the light.

- Hand-held, the slowest safe speed is about 1 divided by the focal length: with
  the 35 mm lens, 1/40 s or faster. Slower than that and the photo comes out
  shaken.
- To freeze a child or a dog that runs: 1/500 s or faster.
- To make a river look like silk: 1 s or more, on a tripod.

## ISO

The sensitivity of the sensor. Each time I double it, the photo gets one stop
brighter and a little noisier. In daylight I keep ISO 100. Indoors or at night,
hand-held, I go to ISO 1600 or 3200 before I accept a shaken photo.
