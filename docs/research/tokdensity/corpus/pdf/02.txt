3. Method
3.1 Data collection
The measurements were taken between March and September of 2024 at
twelve sites along the northern coast, chosen to cover the full range of
exposure from sheltered estuary to open shore. At each site a transect
was laid perpendicular to the waterline and sampled at five-metre inter-
vals from the high-water mark to the lowest point reachable at spring low
tide. Sediment cores of 30 cm were extracted with a stainless-steel cor-
er, sealed on site and transported to the laboratory within six hours.
3.2 Laboratory analysis
Each core was divided into three horizons (0–10, 10–20 and 20–30 cm) and
dried at 60 °C for 48 hours before grain-size analysis by laser diffrac-
tion. Organic content was estimated by loss on ignition at 550 °C. Sub-
samples of 5 g were digested in nitric acid for trace-metal determina-
tion by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry, following the pro-
cedure described by Martínez et al. (2019) with the modifications not-
ed in Appendix B.
3.3 Statistical treatment
Differences between sites and horizons were tested with a two-way analy-
sis of variance after confirming normality with the Shapiro–Wilk test and
homogeneity of variance with Levene's test. Where the assumptions were
not met, the Kruskal–Wallis test was applied instead. Post-hoc compari-
sons used Tukey's honest significant difference at a family-wise error
rate of 0.05. All analyses were performed in R version 4.3.2.
3.4 Limitations
The sampling design does not capture seasonal variation within a single
year, since each site was visited only once. Two of the twelve sites (S7
and S11) could not be sampled at the lowest interval because of unsafe
conditions on the day of the visit, and the corresponding cells are re-
ported as missing rather than interpolated. The laser-diffraction meth-
od tends to underestimate the clay fraction relative to sieve-and-pipette
methods, so absolute values should be compared with earlier studies with
caution, although relative comparisons within this dataset are unaffect-
ed.
