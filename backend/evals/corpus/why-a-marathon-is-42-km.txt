Why a marathon is 42 kilometres

The legend says that a Greek messenger, Pheidippides, ran from the battlefield
of Marathon to Athens to announce a victory, and died after he gave the
message. The first modern Olympic Games, in Athens in 1896, had a race of about
40 kilometres to remember him.

The distance changed from race to race until the London Olympics of 1908. The
route went from Windsor Castle to the royal box of the stadium, and it measured
42.195 kilometres. In 1921 that distance became the official one.

A half marathon is half of it: 21.0975 kilometres. It is the most popular race
distance in Spain, because it needs less training than a marathon and is still
a long race.
