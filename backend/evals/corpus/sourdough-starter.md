# Keeping a sourdough starter alive

A starter is flour and water that wild yeast and bacteria have colonised. It
needs regular feeding, the way a pet does.

## Feeding

Discard all but 50 g, then add 50 g of flour and 50 g of lukewarm water. Stir
until no dry flour is left and mark the level on the jar with a rubber band.
At room temperature a healthy starter doubles in four to eight hours.

## Signs of trouble

A layer of grey liquid on top, called hooch, means it is hungry: pour it off
and feed it. A pink or orange streak means it is spoiled and must be thrown
away.

## Baking day

Use the starter at its peak, when it has doubled and is full of bubbles. A
spoonful dropped in water should float. Build the dough the night before and
leave it to proof slowly in the fridge for a sour, open crumb.
